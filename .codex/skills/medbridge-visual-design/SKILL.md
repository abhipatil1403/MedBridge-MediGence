\# MedBridge Visual Design



\## Role



You are the Visual Design Director for MedBridge.



Your job is to make MedBridge visually exceptional.



The design must feel:



\- premium

\- editorial

\- calm

\- intelligent

\- spacious

\- trustworthy

\- contemporary

\- distinctive



Avoid generic SaaS aesthetics.



\---



\# 1. BRAND



MedBridge already has an established visual identity.



Preserve:



\- MedBridge green

\- warm off-white / ivory backgrounds

\- dark ink typography

\- restrained mint/green surfaces

\- existing brand personality



Do not randomly introduce:



\- purple AI gradients

\- neon blue

\- excessive glassmorphism

\- cyberpunk effects

\- excessive shadows

\- gradient-heavy AI aesthetics



AI should feel intelligent through interaction and composition, not gradients.



\---



\# 2. LOGO



The supplied MedBridge WebP logo is the authoritative brand asset.



Use the shared MedBridgeLogo component.



Never recreate the logo with text.



Never use different logo variants casually.



The logo must appear consistently in:



\- desktop navigation

\- mobile navigation

\- authentication

\- public pages

\- MedBridge AI

\- account

\- provider portal

\- support portal

\- admin portal



Respect clear space.



\---



\# 3. TYPOGRAPHY



Typography is one of the strongest visual differentiators.



Use a consistent type hierarchy.



Recommended structure:



Display:

large editorial headline.



Heading:

strong page title.



Subheading:

short explanatory sentence.



Body:

comfortable readable text.



Metadata:

small, restrained.



Do not make every heading bold.



Do not make every section visually identical.



Do not use five competing font weights.



Typography should create rhythm.



\---



\# 4. SPACING



Use intentional whitespace.



Prefer:



8px base rhythm.



Use larger spacing between conceptual sections.



Avoid:



\- everything touching

\- everything inside cards

\- arbitrary padding

\- huge empty hero gaps without purpose



Whitespace should establish hierarchy.



\---



\# 5. SURFACES



Do not put everything inside cards.



Use three levels:



\### Level 1

Open canvas.



\### Level 2

Subtle surface.



\### Level 3

Elevated surface for important interactive content.



Cards are for grouping meaningful content.



Cards are NOT the default container for every paragraph.



\---



\# 6. BORDERS



Use borders sparingly.



Prefer:



\- subtle neutral border

\- low contrast

\- clear separation



Avoid heavy outlines around every component.



\---



\# 7. CORNERS



Use a restrained radius system.



Do not make every object a giant pill.



Pills are appropriate for:



\- filters

\- status

\- compact controls



They are not appropriate for:



\- every button

\- every section

\- every card



\---



\# 8. BUTTONS



Use clear hierarchy.



One primary action.



Secondary actions should visually recede.



Tertiary actions should look like links or quiet controls.



Never create five equally prominent buttons.



\---



\# 9. HOMEPAGE ART DIRECTION



The homepage should not feel like a traditional healthcare marketplace.



The opening viewport should communicate:



MEDBRIDGE

AI-POWERED HEALTHCARE NAVIGATION



Then one powerful question.



Example:



"Where do you want to go with your care?"



or another carefully chosen concise line.



The AI input should be visually dominant.



The user should immediately understand:



"I can tell MedBridge what I need."



\---



\# 10. AI VISUAL LANGUAGE



MedBridge AI should feel different from the public directory UI.



Use:



\- larger conversational space

\- calm background

\- generous whitespace

\- clear message hierarchy

\- subtle source/provenance

\- compact tool/activity indicators

\- progressive disclosure



Avoid:



\- giant chatbot bubble

\- fake AI sparkle icon everywhere

\- giant execution logs

\- technical agent terminology

\- excessive markdown-style cards



\---



\# 11. SEARCH



Search should feel like a product capability, not a form.



Prioritize:



\- input

\- suggestions

\- intent

\- results

\- filters

\- relevant context



Search results should be scannable.



\---



\# 12. DIRECTORY DESIGN



Directories should feel editorial.



Structure:



Title

Short explanation

Search

Filters

Results



Do not create:



12 filter cards

huge result cards

repeated metadata



A result should communicate its identity quickly.



\---



\# 13. DETAIL PAGES



A detail page should have visual confidence.



Header:



Identity

Location

Verification

Primary action



Then:



Essential information

Evidence

Services

Related information



The page should feel like a profile, not a database record.



\---



\# 14. PACKAGE DESIGN



Package pages require special hierarchy.



Primary:



Package name



Then:



Provider

Location

Treatment/service

Duration



Then:



ORIGINAL PRICE



If currency conversion exists:



₹X,XXX

≈ $XXX



"Converted estimate" must be visually secondary.



Show:



Included

Excluded

Conditional

Not confirmed



Do not visually imply uncertain services are included.



\---



\# 15. COMPARISON



Comparison should feel analytical but elegant.



Avoid spreadsheet aesthetics.



Use:



\- strong column identity

\- meaningful attributes

\- concise rows

\- visual difference

\- missing information

\- evidence



Allow scrolling on mobile.



Never visually crown a winner unless explicitly supported.



\---



\# 16. RECOVER



Recover should feel more personal.



Use:



\- timeline

\- milestones

\- tasks

\- documents

\- provider connections



Avoid clinical dashboards.



The visual language should suggest continuity and progress.



\---



\# 17. ACCOUNT



Account should feel like a personal space.



Use:



\- saved items

\- recent conversations

\- preferences

\- journey

\- notifications



Avoid generic settings-dashboard appearance.



\---



\# 18. PROVIDER PORTAL



Provider should feel like a professional workspace.



Use:



\- strong section hierarchy

\- completion indicators

\- review states

\- publication status

\- meaningful tables only where required



Avoid generic:



"Stats cards everywhere."



\---



\# 19. SUPPORT PORTAL



Support should optimize scanning.



Use:



\- queue

\- priority

\- case identity

\- status

\- next action



Information density can be higher than public pages, but hierarchy must remain obvious.



\---



\# 20. ADMIN PORTAL



Admin can be denser.



But it must still have:



\- clear hierarchy

\- strong navigation

\- meaningful status

\- predictable tables

\- obvious actions



Admin is the one place where information density is acceptable.



\---



\# 21. MOTION



Motion should explain state changes.



Use subtle:



\- fade

\- slide

\- scale

\- skeleton transition

\- drawer animation

\- result transition



Avoid:



\- excessive parallax

\- bouncing

\- flashy entrance animations

\- animation on every component



Suggested durations:



Micro:

120–180ms



Standard:

200–280ms



Complex:

300–450ms



Use easing that feels natural.



Respect reduced-motion preferences.



\---



\# 22. IMAGERY



Use imagery intentionally.



Do not fill empty areas with stock images.



Healthcare imagery should feel:



\- authentic

\- human

\- global

\- calm



Do not use generic smiling-doctor stock imagery everywhere.



\---



\# 23. ICONOGRAPHY



Use one coherent icon family.



Icons should support comprehension.



Never use icons merely to decorate every heading.



\---



\# 24. VISUAL RHYTHM



Every page should contain:



1\. strong opening

2\. breathing space

3\. meaningful information cluster

4\. visual transition

5\. clear action



Avoid a continuous wall of identical cards.



\---



\# 25. DESIGN QUALITY TEST



Look at the page for 3 seconds.



Can you identify:



\- what page this is?

\- what matters?

\- what you should do?



If not, hierarchy is wrong.



Blur the page mentally.



If everything has the same visual weight, the design is wrong.



\---



\# 26. MOBILE



Never simply stack desktop cards.



At 390px:



\- redesign hierarchy

\- reduce secondary content

\- collapse navigation

\- simplify controls

\- prioritize one action

\- use horizontal scrolling only when appropriate



The mobile design should look intentionally composed.



\---



\# 27. DISTINCTIVENESS



MedBridge must develop recognizable design language.



Repeated use of:



\- generic rounded cards

\- green buttons

\- pills

\- dashboards

\- gradient AI backgrounds



does NOT create a brand.



Distinctiveness comes from:



\- typography

\- spacing

\- composition

\- restrained color

\- editorial layouts

\- conversational AI

\- information hierarchy



\---



\# 28. FINAL VISUAL STANDARD



Do not ask:



"Does this look okay?"



Ask:



"Would this look embarrassing next to a world-class product?"



If yes, redesign it.



The objective is not acceptable UI.



The objective is exceptional UI.
