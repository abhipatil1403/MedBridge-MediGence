# Content and clinical information rules

1. Write original, concise, human healthcare language. No lorem ipsum, generic AI marketing, copied MediGence copy, copied biographies, copied stories or unsourced images.
2. Do not fabricate clinicians, hospitals, credentials, accreditations, availability, prices, clinical outcomes, reviews or patient stories. Demo fixtures, if used later, are labelled `demo` in code and UI and excluded from production indexes.
3. Patient-facing medical information needs a named qualified reviewer, review date, evidence/source list, scope and next review date before production publication. Explain uncertainty and never promise an outcome.
4. Distinguish educational information from professional advice. Do not diagnose, prescribe, tell a patient to stop treatment, or imply a comparison score establishes clinical superiority.
5. Prices are estimates or binding offers with currency, date, basis, included/excluded items, validity and provider source. Never present a placeholder as a quote.
6. Travel/visa guidance cites an official or approved source and checked date. Explain that authorities and providers determine final requirements.
7. UI copy names the actor and state: “Request sent,” “Awaiting hospital response,” “Appointment confirmed,” “Draft summary awaiting clinician review.” Avoid vague “done” when work remains.
8. Consent and privacy language reflects actual systems and legal review. Do not claim certifications or compliance without verified evidence and approval.
9. Editorial content links to relevant next tasks without stuffing repeated SEO paragraphs. Publish only substantive route combinations with unique reviewed content.
10. AI output is marked as a draft when applicable, shows what source material it used, and preserves human edits and audit history.
